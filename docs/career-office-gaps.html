Gaps versus Handshake’s career office

Handshake’s staff product is an operations desk. Ours is an insights shell with a thin ops layer. Advisors open Handshake to clear queues. They open ours to read a board pack.

Mental model

Handshake organizes around queues: pending jobs, pending employers, today’s appointments, upcoming fairs. We organize around reports: placement rate, engagement, equity flag. Advisors come in to process work, not to read a board pack. Home should be the queue; Reports should be the board pack.

Flow, not features

Handshake daily loop

  1. Open pending jobs. Approve, decline, or request more info. Auto-rules eat the obvious ones.
  2. Scan today’s appointments and drop-ins. Notes stay on the student.
  3. Check fair / event registrations and employer booth questions.
  4. Pull a student list or employer record only when doing outreach.
  5. Insights is a separate mode for the director and institutional reporting.

Our daily loop today

  1. Land on six KPIs and source badges. Primary CTA is “Open action queue.”
  2. Approvals is a title + company row with Approve / Hold. No job body, no “request info,” no auto-approve.
  3. Employers is invite + pending partnership. No notes, flags, or school-level roles.
  4. Advising is a flat request list. Events is a three-field form.
  5. Actions duplicates approvals, events, and nudges. Reports splits the same snapshot across eight URLs.

Information architecture

Handshake (simplified)

Students · Employers · Jobs · Events · Fairs · Appointments · Interviews · Insights · Settings

Objects first. Permissions (Access / Manage) hide write actions. Settings is a real product: approval preferences, appointment types, rooms.

Ours (current)

Home · Approvals · Curated jobs · Employers · Advising · More (4) · Reports (8)

Primary set is the right instinct. Reports still has eight peers that are one dashboard with different charts. Actions is a junk drawer. staffRole is cosmetic.

Where we should not copy Handshake

Handshake’s student surface is search. Ours is swipe. Campus curated as the default Discover stack is the correct career-office lever — one collection membership changes what students see first. Handshake collections are optional filters on a search product.

Swipe right vs pass by category is a demand signal Handshake does not have. Keep it on Home as an operations insight (“students want X, we have Y jobs”), not as another report page.

Do not recreate Handshake’s 50-report catalog. One Insights mode with saved exports (NACE / HEC) is enough until a director asks for scheduled email.

Close the gap in this order

Priority Change Why it matches staff reality
P0 Make Home an inbox: pending jobs, pending employers, today’s advising, students needing a nudge. Advisors process queues. Directors can still open Reports.
P0 Job-based approval like Handshake: one queue, job body visible, approve-job-approves-employer, Hold with a reason. Stops Approvals and Actions from being two copies of the same decision.
P1 Employer record: notes, status history, invite + partnership on one profile. Kill the three stacked lists. Employer relations is a CRM job, not a form job.
P1 Advising calendar: staff identity, type, confirm/reschedule. Student already books; the desk is the hole. This is the actual in-office work Handshake owns.
P2 Events with RSVP counts and a student list. Fairs/booths later. A datetime form is not an event product.
P2 Collapse eight report routes into Insights. Keep NACE export. Staff roles that actually hide write actions. Matches Handshake’s Access vs Manage without copying their report zoo.

Design tradeoff

Copying Handshake’s full career-center suite is a multi-year product. Winning as a campus tool means being better at the two things they are weak at (swipe curation + demand from passes) and being competent at the three things staff will not switch without (job queue, employer record, advising desk).
